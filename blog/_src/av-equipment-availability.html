To stop double-booking AV gear, keep one source of truth for every item, block each booking from load-out to check-in (not just show dates), and confirm availability before you send a quote, not after the client says yes. Most double bookings happen because two people, or two spreadsheets, each think a piece of gear is free.

Here is where overlaps actually come from, a simple booking window rule, and a short routine that catches conflicts before the truck is loaded.

Where double bookings come from

It is rarely carelessness. It is usually one of these:

  • Two lists. One person quotes from a spreadsheet and another books from a calendar, and neither sees the other's entries.
  • Quoting before checking. A quote goes out with gear on it, the client approves a week later, and by then the gear has been promised elsewhere.
  • Show dates only. The calendar says Saturday, but the gear leaves Thursday and is not back on the shelf until Monday.
  • Quantity blindness. You own 6 wireless mics, one show books 4, another books 4, and each looks fine on its own.
  • Late returns. A rental that comes back a day late quietly collides with the next booking.

Block the real window, not the show date

The fix that matters most is changing what you block on the calendar. A booking should hold the gear from the day it leaves until the day it is back and ready, not only the days the client uses it.

Part of the windowWhat it covers
PrepPulling, testing, labeling and packing the gear
OutDelivery, load-in, show days, strike, return trip
TurnaroundCheck-in, inspection, cleaning, repairs

The turnaround block is the one most shops skip. A return is not finished when the case lands on your dock. For example, one university equipment lab states that returns are complete only once all items have been inspected and verified. The same logic applies to a rental shop: until a cable, battery or lens has been checked, it is not ready to promise to the next job.

Count quantities, not just items

Availability is a number, not a yes or no. For each item, keep three figures in view: how many you own, how many are committed on the dates in question, and how many are in repair or otherwise out of service. Available is the first minus the other two.

A worked example with made-up numbers: you own 8 wireless handhelds, 1 is in for repair, and one show on the 14th–16th has 4 reserved. A new request for 4 on the 15th looks fine on the surface. Available is 8 minus 1 minus 4, which is 3. You are one short, so you need to sub-rent one, offer a different model, or move the dates.

Check before you quote, hold when you send

Run this routine on every quote that has gear on it:

  1. Check the full window (prep to turnaround) for every line with limited stock.
  2. Mark the quote as a soft hold in your calendar when you send it, with an expiry date. Put that date on the quote.
  3. Convert to a firm booking when the client approves, and release the hold if the quote expires or is declined.
  4. Re-check after every revision. A change in dates or quantities is a new availability question.
  5. Close the loop at check-in. Mark the gear back in stock only after it has been inspected.

If a conflict shows up, deal with it early. Your options, in the order most shops try them: swap for an equivalent item, sub-rent the shortfall (see our post on quoting gear you don't own), shift prep or turnaround times, or tell the client before they approve, not after.

Plan for late returns and swaps

Even a clean calendar fails when gear comes back late or breaks on site. Two habits help. First, write a late-return rule into your rental terms, such as an extra day billed at your day-2 rate, so clients have a reason to return on time. Second, avoid booking your last unit of a critical item back to back with another job. If the gear has to go straight from one show to the next, say so on the quote and confirm the return time with the first client the day before.

Keep a short list of backup options for your busiest items: an equivalent model you own, a partner shop you can sub-rent from, or a lower-spec item you are willing to offer. When a conflict appears, you will be choosing from a list instead of improvising the night before load-out.

Questions

How do you stop double-booking AV equipment?

Keep one list of gear and bookings, block each booking from prep through check-in, count quantities per date range, and check availability before sending a quote. Re-check whenever the quote is revised.

Should you hold gear for a quote the client hasn't approved?

Yes, but as a soft hold with an expiry date written on the quote. If the client has not approved by then, release the gear so it can be booked by someone else.

How much turnaround time should you leave between rentals?

It depends on the gear. Cables and stands need little; cases of wireless, video and lighting gear need testing and sometimes repair. Set a turnaround block for each category and apply it to every booking.
