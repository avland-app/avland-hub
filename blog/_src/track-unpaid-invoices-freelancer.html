The simplest way to track unpaid invoices as an AV freelancer is a one-page list with five columns: invoice number, company, amount, due date and status. Update it every time you send an invoice or get paid, and look at it once a week. Most freelancers don't lose money because a company refuses to pay. They lose it because an invoice quietly slipped past its due date and nobody noticed for six weeks.

Here's what to put in that list, how to sort it by age, what to do on your weekly check, and how to handle an invoice that's stuck.

What your tracker needs (and nothing more)

A spreadsheet, a notes file or a page in a notebook all work. What matters is that every invoice is on it from the day you send it, not from the day you start worrying.

ColumnWhat to write
Invoice #The number on the PDF, so you and accounts payable talk about the same document
Company and contactWho booked you and who receives invoices (often two different people)
AmountThe full total, including expenses
Sent and due datesThe date you emailed it, and the date it's due under your terms
StatusSent, confirmed received, scheduled, paid, or disputed

The "confirmed received" status is the one most people skip. An invoice that was emailed is not the same as an invoice that arrived. If a company hasn't acknowledged it within a couple of days, a one-line "Just confirming this reached the right person" costs you nothing and removes the most common cause of a stuck payment.

Sort by age, not by date sent

A list sorted by date sent tells you what you did. A list sorted by how late each invoice is tells you what to do next. Group everything unpaid into these buckets:

  • Not due yet. Leave it alone. Chasing before the due date annoys people who are about to pay you on time.
  • 1 to 7 days past due. Probably stuck in a queue. A short, friendly email is enough.
  • 8 to 30 days past due. Time to call the person who booked you and email accounts payable at the same time.
  • Over 30 days past due. Written notice, and no new gigs from that company until it's settled.

These cutoffs are a suggestion, not a rule. If your terms are Net 15, the buckets should be tighter. The follow-up wording for each stage is in payment terms that get AV freelancers paid faster, so this guide sticks to the tracking side.

Do a ten-minute check every week

A tracker only works if you look at it. Pick a fixed time, such as Monday morning, and run the same short routine:

  1. Mark what was paid. Check your bank for deposits and update the list. Payments often arrive without any email.
  2. Find anything newly past due. Sort by due date and look at the top.
  3. Send the day's follow-ups. One email or call per overdue invoice, and write the date you sent it in the status column.
  4. Add the invoices you owe yourself. If you worked a show last week and haven't invoiced, that's the first unpaid invoice on your list.

That last step matters more than it looks. Late invoicing is a late payment, because the clock doesn't start until the invoice lands.

When an invoice is stuck, find out why first

Most overdue invoices have a boring reason. Ask about these before assuming the worst:

  • Missing vendor setup. The company never got your W-9 or payment details.
  • Missing PO or job number. Accounts payable can't match the invoice to a budget.
  • Wrong recipient. It went to the person who booked you, not the person who pays.
  • A question about a line. Often an expense without a receipt. See how to bill parking, travel and kit fees.

Write the reason in your status column. If the same company stalls for the same reason twice, that's a pattern you can fix before the next gig, for example by asking for the PO number when you accept the booking.

If an invoice goes well past due with no explanation, check what your written agreement says before you escalate. California, for example, has a freelancer law. According to the office of the senator who authored it, a freelancer doing more than $250 of work over a four-month period is entitled to a written contract, and clients must pay within 30 days of completion unless the contract says otherwise. The same page says freelancers who aren't paid can be entitled to damages plus costs and attorney's fees. That's a summary from the bill's author, not the statute itself, and other states differ, so read the actual law for your situation and ask a lawyer before you act on it.

Questions

How do I keep track of unpaid invoices as a freelancer?

Keep one list with invoice number, company, amount, due date and status. Add each invoice the day you send it, sort by how overdue it is, and review it once a week.

When is an invoice officially overdue?

When the due date in your agreement has passed. If you stated "Net 15" or "Net 30" from the invoice date, the due date is that many days after you sent it. If no due date was agreed, ask which date applies before you follow up.

How soon should I follow up on a late invoice?

A few days after the due date for a first, friendly check. Earlier is fine if you never confirmed the invoice arrived.

Should I stop working for a company that pays late?

Many freelancers pause new bookings until the overdue invoice is paid. Decide your rule before the next gig and say it plainly if it comes up.
