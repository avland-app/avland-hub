Put expenses on a freelance AV invoice as their own lines, below your labor, each with a plain label, the amount and a receipt attached when it's a pass-through cost. Bill parking, tolls and materials at cost, bill mileage at a stated per-mile rate, and put your kit fee in your rate or on its own line, but never both. If the company knows how you charge before the gig, expenses stop being a fight after it.

Here's how to separate the types of expenses, word each line so accounts payable approves it, and agree on it up front.

Three kinds of expense, three different lines

Most arguments about expenses come from mixing these together on one line called "misc."

TypeExamplesBill it as
Pass-throughParking, tolls, partsActual cost + receipt
TravelDriving, flight, hotelPer-mile rate or receipts
KitYour mics, laptop, toolsFlat daily fee or in rate

Pass-through costs are the easy ones. You spent the money for the job, so the company pays it back at cost. Don't add a markup unless you agreed to one in advance.

Parking, tolls and small purchases

Write each one as its own line with the date and what it was: "Parking, Mar 12, venue garage, $34.00." Attach a photo of the receipt in the same email, or in the same PDF if you can. A line that can be checked in five seconds gets approved. A line that says "Expenses $112" gets a reply asking what it is.

Take the receipt photo the same day. Thermal paper fades, and it's much easier to match a receipt to a job while you remember which job it was.

For anything bigger than parking, such as buying a part so the show could go on, tell the person who booked you at the time if you can. A text saying "grabbed a replacement cable for $18, will put it on the invoice" turns a surprise line into an expected one.

Mileage and travel

For driving, you have two options: bill actual costs, or bill a per-mile rate. The per-mile rate is simpler, and it's what most companies expect for local driving. The IRS publishes a standard business mileage rate that it updates, and it's a reasonable reference point when you and a company agree on a number. Check the IRS standard mileage rates page for the current figure rather than copying last year's.

Whatever rate you use, show the math on the invoice so nobody has to ask: "Mileage, Oakland to venue and back, 38 miles at the agreed per-mile rate." Say whether you charge from your home or from the shop. If a company has its own travel policy, ask for it before you book.

For flights and hotels, bill the real cost with receipts, and get approval first. Never book travel the company hasn't agreed to and then invoice it.

Mileage and expense reimbursements have tax rules of their own for both sides, and they depend on your situation. That's a question for your accountant, not for an invoice template.

Your kit fee: charge it once

If you bring your own gear, you can build it into your day rate or bill it as a separate line. Both work. What doesn't work is quoting a day rate that already includes your kit and then adding a kit fee on top, because that's the line that gets questioned.

Pick one approach and say it in your booking message:

  • Kit included: "$650/10 hours, my laptop and standard tools included."
  • Kit separate: "$550/10 hours plus $75/day for my mic kit."

The separate line is useful when the kit changes from job to job. If you carry a wireless mic package on one show and nothing on the next, a per-job kit fee matches what you actually brought. Where expenses go on the invoice

Keep the layout the same every time:

  1. Labor lines first: date, call and out times, rate, total.
  2. Kit fee next, if you charge one.
  3. Expenses below, one line each, with a receipt.
  4. Subtotals for labor and expenses, then the grand total.

Questions

Should I mark up expenses on a freelance invoice?

Only if you agreed to it before the job. The usual practice for pass-through costs like parking, tolls and parts is to bill them at cost with a receipt.

Can I charge a kit fee on top of my day rate?

Only if you told the company your day rate didn't include your kit. If your quoted rate already covers your gear, adding a kit fee on top is double billing.

How should I bill mileage as a freelance AV tech?

Use a per-mile rate you stated before the job, show the miles and the rate on the invoice, and say where you start from. The IRS standard mileage rate is a common reference point.

Do I need receipts for small expenses?

Attach one for every pass-through cost, even parking. It's what lets accounts payable approve the line without a back-and-forth.
