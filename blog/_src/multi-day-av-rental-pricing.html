The cleanest way to price a multi-day AV rental is to charge the full rate for day 1, set a fixed rule for every day after that, and cap long rentals with a weekly multiplier. Many shops charge day 2 and 3 at a lower percentage of the day-1 rate, because the gear is already loaded, prepped and on site. What matters most is that the rule is written down before the client asks.

Here are the common structures, a worked three-day example with made-up numbers, and how to put the rule on a quote so nobody argues about it at the end of the show.

Why day 2 and 3 usually cost less than day 1

Your cost to rent a piece of gear is not spread evenly over the days. Most of the work happens once: pulling it, testing it, cabling it, loading the truck, delivering it, and getting it back and checked in. Day 2 and day 3 add wear and keep the item out of other bookings, but they don't add another prep and delivery cycle.

That is the whole argument for a lower rate on later days. It's also the limit: gear that sits on your shelf earning nothing is not the same as gear locked in a client's ballroom, so the discount should never drop below what it costs you to keep that item unavailable.

Flat per dayEvery day at the same rateHigh-demand gear, one-day events, simple quotes

Multiplier tiersTotal = day rate × a set number: e.g. a week costs 3.5 daysLong rentals, install support, touring and conference weeks

The tier approach is common outside AV rental houses too. A worked example

These numbers are invented to show the math. A video processor rents for $400 on day 1. Your rule: day 2 and day 3 at 60% of the day-1 rate, and a week capped at 3.5 times the day rate.

Rental lengthCalculationTotal
1 day$400$400
2 days$400 + $240$640
3 days$400 + $240 + $240$880
7 days (weekly cap, 3.5x)$400 × 3.5$1,400

A three-day show costs the client about 2.2 times the day-1 rate. A full week costs 3.5 times. Without the cap, seven days at 60% per extra day would come to $1,840. The cap is where you decide that a week-long rental is a different deal.

Decide what counts as a "day"

Most multi-day arguments are not about the discount. They are about the count. Put these in your terms:

  • The clock. Is a day 24 hours from pickup, or a calendar day? Gear delivered Thursday at 4 p.m. and struck Saturday at noon is two or three days depending on your answer.
  • Load-in and strike days. If the gear is on site the day before the show for setup, does that day count? Most shops say yes if the client has the gear, even if it isn't used.
  • Weekends. Some shops don't charge Saturday and Sunday separately when the gear goes out Friday and returns Monday. Others do. Pick one.
  • Dark days. If the show pauses a day between sessions and the gear stays on site, it still counts.
  • Late returns. Say what a late return costs, for instance another full day at your day-2 rate.

How to show it on the quote

Clients accept a multi-day rule when they can see it. Keep three things visible:

  • The number of rental days on each equipment line, next to the rate and the total.
  • A note that states the rule in one sentence, such as "Day 1 at full rate; each additional day at 60%."
  • The dates, so the client can see exactly which days you counted.

If the schedule changes, update the days and send a new revision rather than adjusting the total by hand. For where rental days and rates sit on the quote itself, see our guide to writing an AV quote.

Questions

How do you price day 2 and day 3 of an AV rental?

Charge the full rate on day 1, then a set percentage of it on each extra day, often 50% to 75% depending on how fast the gear can be rebooked. Write the percentage on the quote.

Should every item get the same multi-day discount?

No. Gear that is in constant demand can stay at a flat day rate, while specialty gear that rarely goes out can carry a deeper discount to win the booking. Group your items and set a rule for each group.

What is a weekly rate for AV equipment?

A weekly rate is a cap on the total for a seven-day rental, written as a multiple of the day rate. Some published schedules use 3.5 times the day rate; yours should reflect how often the item is booked.
