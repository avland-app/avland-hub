How to quote AV crew positions (A1, V1, L1, TD)

December 7, 2026 · 6 min read

To quote AV crew positions, list each role by its job title (A1, V1, L1, TD), give each one a day rate that covers a stated number of hours, say what the role does on your show, and add overtime terms in the notes. A client who sees "A1, 10-hour day, $650" knows what they are buying. A client who sees "Audio tech, 1 day" has to ask.

Below: what the common positions mean, how to price each one, how to write them on a quote so the crew count holds up when the client pushes back, and how to keep your rates consistent from job to job.

What the common AV positions mean

Titles vary by company and city, so write the plain-English duty next to the abbreviation the first time a client sees it. The ones most quotes need:

PositionShort nameWhat they run on a show
Audio engineerA1Mixes the show: mics, playback, program audio. Usually runs the main console.
Audio assistantA2Mics and wireless on talent, patching, stage-side audio fixes.
Video engineerV1Switching, scaling, processors, projectors and LED wall signal.
Graphics or playback operatorV2 / GFXRuns slides, lower thirds, video playback and cues.
Lighting tech or designerL1Programs and runs the lighting console, or sets the rig.
Technical directorTDOwns the technical plan, the schedule and the crew, and talks to the client's producer.

These are common usages, not a standard. Some shops call the second audio person "A2," others call them "stage tech." Pick your names and use them on every quote.

The AVIXA write-up of an InfoComm 2026 session on live event roles notes that on-site teams add positions once an event is underway, including show callers, deck managers, graphics operators, audio engineers, video engineers and technical managers. That is a good reminder that the crew list on a quote should match the show, not a template.

How to price each position

Price every position the same way, so your quotes stay consistent:

  1. Set a day rate for each role. A TD and a stagehand are not the same price, and a V1 who can build an LED wall costs more than a V2 running slides.
  2. State the hours the day rate covers. Ten hours is common. Everything after that follows your overtime terms. (How to calculate overtime for AV crew calls has the math.)
  3. Decide how you bill prep and travel. Pre-light, load-in, tech rehearsal and strike are often separate days or half days.
  4. Add your markup. The rate you pay a freelancer is a cost. The rate on the quote includes your margin and the risk you carry.

How to write crew on a quote

Crew is where clients cut first, because it is the line they cannot see on stage. Make each position easy to defend:

  • Name the duty, not just the title. "A1: mixes the general session, 6 wireless mics and playback" is harder to cut than "A1."
  • Show days and hours. "Load-in 6 a.m., show 9 a.m. to 3 p.m., strike to 6 p.m." tells the client why it is a 12-hour day, not a 10.
  • Separate prep from show days. The client sees what they can move or drop.
  • Say what happens if they cut a position. If the A2 comes off, someone else carries the wireless. Write that into the notes so the cut is a decision, not a surprise.
  • List overtime terms once, in the notes. What the day covers, the overtime and double-time multiples, and who approves going long.

Mistakes that cost money

  • Forgetting the TD. Someone is coordinating the crew and the client anyway. If you don't quote the role, you pay for it in your own time.
  • One rate for every role. A flat "tech" rate underprices your best people and overprices the entry-level ones.
  • Quoting show hours only. Load-in, check and strike belong on the crew lines, or the overtime lands on you.
  • No lunch or meal terms. If a long day needs meals, decide who provides them before the call.
  • Changing titles between quotes. If "L1" becomes "lighting op" on the next quote, the client wonders whether the role changed.

Check each crew line against the show schedule before you send. If a position has no duty you can name, it probably does not belong on the quote.

Questions

What does A1 mean in AV?

A1 is the lead audio engineer, the person who mixes the show. The A2 is the audio assistant who handles mics, wireless and stage-side fixes. Exact titles vary by company, so define them on your quote.

How many crew positions does a corporate event need?

It depends on the room, the program and the gear. A small meeting may need one tech. A general session with wireless mics, a switcher, lighting and presenters usually needs an A1, a V1, a lighting operator and a TD, and often a graphics operator.

Should crew be quoted as a day rate or hourly?

Most AV quotes use a day rate that covers a stated number of hours, with overtime after that. It is easier for the client to compare and for you to staff. Hourly works for short calls.

Do I mark up freelancer rates on a quote?

Most AV companies do. The rate you pay is a cost, and the quoted rate also covers your scheduling, your risk and your margin. Mark up labor consistently so a long day does not eat your profit.
