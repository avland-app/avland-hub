From approved install quote to material list

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

To turn an approved AV install quote into a material list, go room by room and list every part that is not on the equipment line: cable, connectors, mounts, plates, power, rack hardware, labels and consumables. Then add a spares allowance and check the list against the site walk notes before anything is ordered.

The big items never get forgotten. The $2 parts that stop an install at 3 p.m. on day two do. Here is a way to build the list so the small stuff is on it.

Start from the approved quote, not from memory

The quote is what the client agreed to pay for, so it is the only starting point. Copy every equipment line into the list first, with quantity and room. Then work through each device and ask one question: what does this need to get mounted, powered, connected and labeled?

Doing it device by device is slower than listing "misc cable and hardware," but it is how you catch that the display needs a recessed power box, or that the DSP needs two more rack screws than the shelf came with. If your quote follows the five parts per room, the materials line is where this list gets its numbers.

Walk each device through five questions

QuestionParts that come out of it
How is it mounted?Wall or ceiling mount, backing, fasteners, anchors, rack ears, shelves
How is it powered?Power cords, outlets or boxes, strips, surge or UPS, PoE injectors or switch ports
How is it connected?Cable by type and length, connectors, wall plates, adapters, patch cables
How is it routed?Conduit, J-hooks, cable ties, hook-and-loop straps, firestop, cable pathways
How is it finished?Labels, cable markers, heat shrink, blanks, trim, touch-up paint

The items that get forgotten

These are the lines that most often go missing from an install list, because each one is small and nobody owns it:

  • Wall plate inserts and blanks. The plate is on the list; the inserts, keystone jacks and blanks to fill the openings are not.
  • Cable lengths with service loops. Measure the run, then add slack at each end for terminations and for the next tech who has to pull a device out.
  • Adapters and dongles. A client laptop at the table needs the right input for the connector the room actually has.
  • Rack hardware. Screws, cage nuts, blanks, vent panels, power distribution and a way to label it all.
  • Firestop and sealant. Any hole through a rated wall needs to be closed up properly. Check what the building requires.
  • Labels and markers. Both ends of every cable and every device, matched to your drawing.
  • Consumables. Ties, straps, tape, drill bits, hole saws and blades you will use up on this job.

Match cable to where it runs

Cable is the one material with a rule behind it. Cable that runs through a ceiling used as return air, or through ducts, has to be a type listed for that space. EC&M's explainer on plenum datacom wiring notes that, of the communications cable types in the NEC table, only the plenum-listed types (CMP and MPP) are listed for ducts, plenums and other environmental air-handling spaces, and points to NEC section 300.22 for those spaces. That article is from 2003 and the code has been revised many times since, so check the current edition and your local inspector before you buy.

The practical step: note the ceiling type for each room during the site visit, and put the cable type on the list. Plenum cable costs more, so it needs to be in the quote too, not discovered at the supply house.

Add a spares allowance, then count twice

Connectors fail, crimps get redone and a bad cable turns up in a new box. Add a small stated allowance for the cheap, high-use items (connectors, patch cables, fasteners, ties) based on your last similar job, and keep it separate from the counted quantities so you can see both. For the expensive items, order the exact count.

Then count twice: once against the quote, and once against the site notes. A room that has been renumbered or a ceiling that turned out to be hard-lid changes the list.

Keep one source for your part numbers and prices

The list is only as good as the prices under it. If your connector costs one amount in room 1 and another in room 4, the margin on the job is a guess. FAQ

What goes on an AV installation material list?

Everything needed to mount, power, connect, route and label the equipment: mounts, fasteners, cable, connectors, plates, power items, rack hardware, conduit or pathway, firestop, labels and consumables.

How is a material list different from an equipment list?

The equipment list is the gear the client is buying, such as displays, DSPs and cameras. The material list is the supporting parts that make it work in the building.

How much extra should I order?

There is no standard figure. Use an allowance based on your own past jobs for cheap, high-use parts, and order exact counts for expensive items.

Do I need plenum-rated cable?

If the cable runs through an air-handling space such as a return-air ceiling, it generally has to be a listed plenum type. Confirm with the current electrical code and your local authority.
