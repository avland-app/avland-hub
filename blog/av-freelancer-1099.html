1099 basics for AV freelancers (not tax advice)

November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

If you work AV gigs as a freelancer, most companies pay you as an independent contractor, not as an employee. That means no taxes come out of your pay, you report the income yourself, and you usually owe self-employment tax on top of income tax. The IRS sets that self-employment tax rate at 15.3%. This post covers the basics in plain words. It is general information, not tax advice, so talk to an accountant about your own numbers.

What a 1099 actually is

A 1099 is a form a company may send you to report what it paid you during the year. For contract work that form is usually the 1099-NEC. It is an information form: it tells you and the IRS what a client paid, and it does not calculate what you owe. The dollar amount at which a company has to send you one changes from time to time, so check the current figure on irs.gov instead of trusting a number from a blog.

The key point: you owe tax on all your freelance income whether or not a company sends you a form. A client that paid you under its reporting limit still paid you income. Keep your own records of every gig.

Self-employment tax

When you're an employee, your employer pays half of Social Security and Medicare taxes. When you're a freelancer you pay both halves. The IRS explains that the self-employment tax rate is 15.3%: 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare. The IRS page also says you generally file when your net self-employment earnings reach $400 or more. Net earnings means income minus allowed business expenses, which is one reason to track expenses all year.

Estimated quarterly taxes

Because no one withholds tax from your freelance pay, the IRS says self-employed people may have to pay estimated taxes quarterly, and those payments can cover your self-employment tax too. If you only freelance part of the year, or have a day job as well, the amount you owe can change a lot. An accountant can tell you whether you need to pay quarterly and how much.

A habit many freelancers use: move a share of every payment into a separate savings account the day it arrives, so the tax money isn't spent. What share is right for you is a question for your accountant.

What to track as an AV freelancer

  • Every invoice and payment. Dates, company, amount, invoice number.
  • Expenses you pay yourself. Parking, travel, tools, cables and consumables, insurance, phone. Your accountant decides what counts; you just keep receipts.
  • Mileage. A simple log with date, start and end, and job.
  • W-9s you sent. Companies ask for one when they set you up as a vendor. Know which companies have your details.

Common mistakes freelancers make

Most 1099 trouble is not complicated. It is timing and paperwork.

  • Spending the whole payment. A $600 day rate feels like $600 until tax season. Set money aside as each payment lands.
  • Waiting for a form. A missing 1099 does not make income disappear. Add up your own invoices and compare them with any forms you receive. If a number differs, ask the company before you file.
  • Mixing personal and business spending. One card or account for gig expenses makes receipts easy to find.
  • Losing the paper trail. Save invoices, receipts and the booking message that shows the rate. A photo on your phone counts as a start; a folder per year is better.
  • Treating the first year like the rest. A first-year freelancer with one big job and a full-time employee with a few weekend gigs have very different tax pictures. Check in with an accountant when your mix changes.

Another small habit: when a company books you, ask whether they need a W-9 and send it the same day. Companies that wait for it may hold your first payment, and your payment-terms conversation goes smoother when paperwork is already done. When to talk to an accountant

Early. Before your first big year, ask about quarterly payments, deductible expenses, retirement accounts for the self-employed and whether forming an LLC makes sense in your state. A single hour of advice is cheaper than a surprise bill. State rules differ, so look at your state tax agency as well.

Questions

Do I need a 1099 to report freelance income?

No. Freelance income is taxable whether or not a company sends a form. Keep your own records of every payment.

What is the self-employment tax rate?

The IRS lists it as 15.3%: 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare, applied to net self-employment earnings up to the Social Security wage base.

Do AV freelancers pay quarterly taxes?

Many do, because no tax is withheld from contractor pay. Whether you must, and how much, depends on your income, so ask an accountant.

Is this tax advice?

No. It is general information. Ask an accountant about your situation.
