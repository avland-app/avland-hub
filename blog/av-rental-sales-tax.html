Sales tax on AV rentals and labor: what to check

November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Whether you charge sales tax on an AV rental, and on the labor that goes with it, depends on your state, and sometimes on how the job is written up. There is no single national rule. This guide covers the questions to settle for your own business, with California's published guidance as one worked example. It is general information, not tax advice, so confirm the answers with your accountant or your state tax agency.

Why AV quotes get tricky

A typical AV job mixes several different kinds of charge on one quote: gear rented for a few days, crew time, delivery, setup and strike, sometimes an operator who stays with the system. States can treat each of those differently. A rental of equipment may be taxable while a separately stated installation charge is not, or the reverse. The same dollar amount can be taxed or untaxed depending on what the line is and how it is described.

The practical lesson is to keep each kind of charge on its own line and describe it plainly. You cannot answer a tax question about a lump-sum "AV package" line, and neither can your accountant.

Questions to ask your state

  • Is renting equipment taxable? Many states tax the rental of tangible goods. Some tax the rental receipts, some tax the owner when the gear is bought, some do either.
  • What if an operator comes with the gear? Renting gear with a technician can be treated differently from renting gear alone.
  • Is labor taxable? Crew time, programming and installation labor are treated differently from state to state.
  • What about delivery, setup and strike? Whether these are taxed can depend on who delivers, when, and whether the charge is listed separately.
  • Which tax rate applies? Often it is the rate where the gear is delivered or used, not where your shop is. Local district taxes can change the total.
  • Is the client exempt? Nonprofits, resellers and some government customers may owe no tax, but you generally need the right paperwork on file.

One worked example: California

California's tax department publishes a Tax Guide for Rental Companies. These are the points that matter most for an AV rental house, paraphrased from that page.

Two ways to handle the equipment. By default, a lease of equipment is taxed on the rental receipts, which the guide describes as a continuing sale and purchase. The other option is to pay tax on the purchase price instead. If you pay tax on the purchase price, or timely elect to, tax is not due on the rental receipts, as long as you lease the item in substantially the same form as you acquired it. The guide says the election is made on the return for the period the property first enters rental service, and that it is irrevocable. Which route is better is an accounting decision for each business, not something to guess at.

Operators. The guide excludes from its definition of a lease "a contract that requires that the owner provide an operator with the leased tangible personal property." That means a rental that must come with your technician is not the same thing as a plain gear rental, and it is a good reason to ask your tax agency how your jobs are classified.

Services bundled into the rental. Mandatory services, such as assembly and disassembly, are included in the taxable rental receipts. Optional services that the agreement does not require are not.

Delivery. If you deliver with your own vehicle, the guide says the charge is generally taxable. There are exceptions, for example when delivery happens after the lease begins and the charge is separately stated. The page is explicit that separate statement matters.

That is one agency's guidance on one kind of business. Your state may differ on every point above.

Build the quote so the answer is easy to apply

  1. Separate lines. Gear, crew, programming, delivery, setup and strike each get their own line.
  2. Describe what it is. "Delivery" and "Technician, 8 hours" tell a tax agency more than "Services."
  3. Show a subtotal before tax so the client can see what was taxed.
  4. Keep exemption certificates for clients who do not pay tax, with the invoice they apply to.
  5. Write down your rule once after you have talked to your accountant, and apply it the same way every time.

Questions

Do I charge sales tax on AV equipment rentals?

It depends on your state and how the rental is structured. Many states tax equipment rentals, but the details differ. Check your state tax agency's guide for rental companies and ask your accountant.

Is AV labor taxable?

States differ. Some tax certain labor connected to a rental, others do not, and a separately stated charge is often treated differently from a bundled one. Keep labor on its own line and confirm your state's rule.

Are delivery and setup fees taxable?

Sometimes. In California, for example, delivery in your own vehicle is generally taxable, with exceptions for separately stated charges after the lease starts. Other states have other rules.

Where can I find my state's rules?

Start with your state's department of revenue or tax agency website, look for guides for rental companies or equipment leasing, and confirm anything unclear with an accountant.
