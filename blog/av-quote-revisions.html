Quote revisions: keeping Rev A, B and C straight

November 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Label every version of a quote with a revision letter (Rev A, Rev B, Rev C), keep the old ones untouched, and put a one-line change note on each new one. That way the client always knows which version is current, and you can prove what they approved and when.

Here is a simple revision system for AV quotes, what to write in the change note, and the mistakes that cause arguments on show week.

Why AV quotes get revised so often

An AV quote is almost never final on the first send. The room changes, the headcount grows, the client adds a second screen, drops the recording, or moves the load-in a day earlier. Each change moves the price, the crew count or the gear list.

If you fix the same file and send it again, three things go wrong. The client can't tell what changed. You can't go back to the version they said yes to. And two people end up working from two different numbers.

Use revision letters, not "final_v2_REAL"

Revision letters are an old habit in technical work. Engineering drawing practice, for example, identifies revisions with upper-case letters in sequence starting at A, and says changes are recorded in a revision history block. That is from a summary of ASME Y14.35M on revision of engineering drawings hosted by NDIA. You are not drawing a bracket, but the idea carries over: one letter per version, and a written record of what changed.

A quote number plus a letter does the job. Quote 2481 Rev A is the first send. Rev B is the first change. Nobody has to guess which is newer, and file names like "final", "final2" and "use this one" stop showing up in email threads.

What to put in the change note

Every revision gets a short note at the top of the quote or in the email. Keep it factual: what changed and what it did to the total.

RevisionChange noteTotal effect
Rev AInitial quote: general session, two breakoutsBaseline
Rev BAdded confidence monitors and a second projector in breakout 2Higher
Rev CRemoved recording; load-in moved to the day beforeLower gear, higher labor

The amounts above are left out on purpose. Use your own numbers. The point is that anyone reading the note can see what moved without comparing two PDFs line by line.

Rules that keep revisions clean

  • Never overwrite a sent version. Once Rev A has gone to the client, it stays exactly as sent. Changes go into Rev B.
  • One current revision. Say it in the email: "Rev C replaces Rev B." If the client approves, note which letter they approved.
  • Change what the client asked for, nothing else. Silent price tweaks on other lines are how trust gets lost. If a rate changes, say so in the note.
  • Carry revisions through to the pull sheet and invoice. Work from the approved letter, not from whatever file is open. Our guide to AV pull sheets covers building the list from the approved quote.
  • Re-check availability. A bigger gear list may collide with another show. Look before you send the revision, not after.

Keeping the crew and warehouse on the same letter

The client is not the only person who needs the current version. Your warehouse preps from the gear list, your crew lead reads the labor lines, and your driver needs the load-in time. When a revision lands, tell them which letter replaced which, and what changed for their part of the job.

A short message works: "Quote 2481 is now Rev C. Recording is out, load-in is Tuesday afternoon, no change to the projector count." Put the letter on the pull sheet and on the call sheet too. If the warehouse is still prepping from Rev B on Thursday, you want to find out on Wednesday, not at load-in.

It also helps to decide who is allowed to send a new revision. On a small team that is usually one person. When three people can each tweak and send a version, you end up with two Rev Cs.

Changes after approval

Once the client has approved, treat any further change as a new revision, even a small one. A "quick add" of two wireless mics on a call is still a change to what was agreed. Send the new letter, get the yes in writing, and then do the work. It takes a minute and settles the invoice question before it starts. Questions

What does Rev A, Rev B mean on a quote?

They are revision letters. Rev A is the first version sent to the client, Rev B is the next version after a change, and so on. The highest letter is the current one.

Should I overwrite the old quote or save a new version?

Save a new version. Keep every sent revision as it was sent, so you can show what the client saw and approved at each step.

Do I need a new quote number for each revision?

No. Keep one quote number and change the letter. A new number makes it look like a different job and makes it harder to find the history.

How do I tell the client which version is current?

State it in the email and at the top of the quote: "Rev C replaces Rev B," plus a one-line note on what changed.
